# Sand to Chip: screenplay

*A hand-drawn animated short, about 3 minutes. Draft v2: the reviewer's rewrite adopted on 3 Nov, laid out as a screenplay with continuity notes. For a rewrite, see the notes at the end.*

**Characters.**
- **GRAIN:** curious and expressive; one character through nine forms (sand, quartz, grey silicon, gas, crystal, wafer, coach, die, packaged chip). Same eyes, brows, hands and head tilt in every form.
- **NARRATOR:** warm, precise, quietly amused; explains what the picture cannot.
- **LOU:** an affectionate, meticulous robot factory guide; takes cleanliness and precision seriously.
- **THE TWINS:** two small red oxygen characters, Grain's companions until the furnace.
- **THE CARBON:** two sly black characters who lead the twins away.

**Throughline.** "What could something so small become?" Grain travels left to right through the whole film (screen direction), so every shot hands off to the next in the same direction unless a note says otherwise.

## 0. EXT. BEACH - DAWN

> **Continuity, in:** Grain as a grain of sand, alone.

**0-1** (0.0 to 5.5 s)

A painted beach at dawn; a fingertip lifts a little sand; Grain, one grain among them, peers up with its head tilted.

**NARRATOR**

Big things can begin very small.

**GRAIN**

How small?

**0-2** (5.5 to 10.0 s)

Grain's questioning look held against a quiet sky, then the title.

**NARRATOR**

Small enough to slip between your fingers.

*On screen:* From Sand to Chip; How silicon becomes the heart of our electronics

> **Continuity, out:** Grain is lifted on a fingertip; the title. CUT TO the quarry: the sand's quartz rock, travelling left to right from here on.

## 1. EXT. QUARRY - DAY

> **Continuity, in:** Grain in its quartz form, holding hands with the two oxygen twins.

**1-1** (10.0 to 17.5 s)

From the beach to an ochre quarry with broad terraces; a selected quartz rock is lifted onto a conveyor, Grain riding on it.

**NARRATOR**

Many grains of sand contain quartz. Our factory journey starts with carefully selected quartz rock.

**1-2** (17.5 to 21.5 s)

On the conveyor Grain peers at the rock; a magnifying circle opens over it.

**GRAIN**

What's inside it?

*On screen:* simplified view

**1-3** (21.5 to 25.5 s)

Inside the circle a simplified silicon-and-oxygen arrangement; the oxygen twins hold Grain's hands as the cartoon of the bond.

**NARRATOR**

Silicon and oxygen, chemically joined.

*On screen:* QUARTZ: SiO₂

> **Continuity, out:** The rock rides the conveyor left to right off frame; MATCH: it drops off the conveyor's end into the furnace chute.

## 2. INT. FURNACE HALL - NIGHT, FIRELIGHT

> **Continuity, in:** Grain quartz with the twins; then grey silicon, alone; the carbon characters.

**2-1** (25.5 to 31.0 s)

The furnace in charcoal, burnt orange and cream; the quartz goes in with carbon; Grain tumbles in with its twins and lands; the carbon characters wait.

**NARRATOR**

In a fiercely hot furnace, carbon helps remove the oxygen.

**2-2** (31.0 to 35.0 s)

The carbon characters lead the oxygen twins away as departing wisps of gas; Grain, now grey silicon, calls after them.

**GRAIN**

There go my travelling companions!

**2-3** (35.0 to 40.0 s)

Grain in its grey silicon form looks itself over and notices flecks of unwanted material on its body.

**NARRATOR**

What remains is silicon, but it still needs cleaning.

> **Continuity, out:** The twins leave with the carbon through the vent (exit frame right). Grain, grey, flecked with dirt, is scooped toward the purifier: CUT on Grain looking up at the column.

## 3. INT. PURIFIER - COOL LILAC

> **Continuity, in:** Grain as a wisp of gas; then deposited onto a rod.

**3-1** (40.0 to 46.5 s)

Pale violet, warm white and steel blue: Grain turns into its wisp form, the silicon-containing chemical, rising into a distillation column.

**NARRATOR**

First, silicon is converted into a chemical that can be purified by distillation.

**3-2** (46.5 to 50.5 s)

The wisp rises through the column, the dark flecks left behind on the plates.

**GRAIN**

Leave the unwanted bits behind.

*On screen:* PURIFY

**3-3** (50.5 to 56.0 s)

Glowing heated rods in a chamber visibly thicken as pure silicon deposits on them; Grain's face settles onto one rod.

**NARRATOR**

Then a reaction deposits very pure silicon onto heated rods.

*On screen:* DEPOSIT

> **Continuity, out:** The pure rods are broken into chunks: CUT to the chunks dropping into the crucible.

## 4. INT. CRYSTAL-PULLING HALL

> **Continuity, in:** Grain as the seed, then the growing crystal; a person for scale.

**4-1** (56.0 to 60.0 s)

Chunks of the rods melt in a glowing crucible.

**NARRATOR**

The purified silicon is melted.

**4-2** (60.0 to 66.5 s)

A small seed on a rod touches the melt, then rises and turns slowly; the crystal starts to grow from the contact.

**NARRATOR**

A tiny seed crystal touches the surface, then slowly rises and turns.

**4-3** (66.5 to 72.0 s)

A magnifying circle at the growth front: atoms joining in neat rows; then back to the chamber.

**NARRATOR**

Silicon solidifies onto it, following the seed's orderly atomic pattern.

*On screen:* simplified view

**4-4** (72.0 to 77.0 s)

The wide reveal: the tall ingot hangs from the puller in the hall, its size clear against the machine and a person; Grain's face is on the ingot itself.

**GRAIN**

I've grown.

**NARRATOR**

One continuous crystal: an ingot.

*On screen:* INGOT

> **Continuity, out:** The finished ingot lies down: MATCH CUT from the upright ingot on its cradle to the ingot lying in the saw.

## 5. INT. WAFER WORKSHOP

> **Continuity, in:** Grain as one wafer among many.

**5-1** (77.0 to 81.5 s)

Match cut from the hanging ingot to the ingot lying in the saw; a wire slices thin discs.

**NARRATOR**

The ingot is sliced into thin discs.

**5-2** (81.5 to 86.0 s)

Follow one disc through a short montage: edge ground, polished, cleaned; one broad painted reflection; Grain looks into it, pleased.

**NARRATOR**

Their surfaces are carefully finished, polished, and cleaned.

**5-3** (86.0 to 93.0 s)

The blank wafer enters a protective carrier with others.

**GRAIN**

Am I a chip now?

**NARRATOR**

You're a wafer, the foundation for many chips.

*On screen:* WAFER

> **Continuity, out:** The wafer goes into a carrier with its siblings: the carrier's lid closes, it lifts off frame right.

## 6. INT. FAB, CLEAN ROOM

> **Continuity, in:** Grain inside the carrier; Lou guiding.

**6-1** (93.0 to 97.5 s)

Lou guides the closed carrier into a calm, orderly, blue-green clean room.

**LOU**

Welcome to the fab. Precious cargo coming through!

**6-2** (97.5 to 105.0 s)

An inset enlarges a dust speck beside a delicate pattern; Lou gives the speck a stern look; the inset closes.

**NARRATOR**

Here, tiny patterns become electronic circuits. Even a speck of dust can spoil the work.

*On screen:* simplified view

> **Continuity, out:** Lou opens the carrier at the first tool: CUT INSIDE the tool.

## 7. INT. FAB TOOLS

> **Continuity, in:** Grain the wafer; cross-sections in magnified views; Lou checking.

**7-1** (105.0 to 110.0 s)

Grain in a deposition chamber; a magnified cross-section shows a new thin film forming across the wafer's surface.

**NARRATOR**

Thin films of material are added to the wafer.

*On screen:* DEPOSITION

**7-2** (110.0 to 114.5 s)

The same cross-section: a distinct coating covers the film.

**NARRATOR**

A light-sensitive coating goes on top.

*On screen:* PHOTORESIST

**7-3** (114.5 to 120.5 s)

Light through a patterned mask changes parts of the coating; then the developer opens those areas, as two clear steps.

**NARRATOR**

Light transfers a pattern. Developing opens selected areas in the coating.

*On screen:* LITHOGRAPHY

**7-4** (120.5 to 124.5 s)

The same cross-section: material disappears only under the openings.

**NARRATOR**

Etching removes material through those openings.

*On screen:* ETCH

**7-5** (124.5 to 130.5 s)

A separate magnified view: a controlled, local addition of atoms; Grain watches with interest.

**NARRATOR**

Selected atoms can also be added to change silicon's electrical behaviour.

*On screen:* DOPING

**7-6** (130.5 to 134.5 s)

Lou does three short actions, one per word, inspects the result and gives a small nod.

**LOU**

Clean. Measure. Check.

**7-7** (134.5 to 140.5 s)

The operations again with visible changes building up layer on layer.

**GRAIN**

And again?

**LOU**

Many times. Each pattern has a job.

**7-8** (140.5 to 146.0 s)

The montage resolves into a clear simplified circuit; Grain, satisfied, earns its red coach headband.

**NARRATOR**

Together, these steps form transistors and the connections between them.

> **Continuity, out:** After many layers the wafer is finished; Grain earns its red coach headband (the form from the game): CUT to the wafer rising into the light.

## 8. INT. REVEAL STAGE

**NARRATOR**

*On screen:* DIE

**8-2** (150.5 to 156.0 s)

*On screen:* wafer → die → transistor

## 9. INT. PROBE STATION

> **Continuity, in:** Grain's die among its neighbours on the wafer; Lou.

**9-1** (156.0 to 162.0 s)

**NARRATOR**

**9-2** (162.0 to 166.5 s)

**GRAIN**

**LOU**

**NARRATOR**

*On screen:* Quartz → Silicon → Crystal → Wafer → Chips

## Notes for a rewrite

- **Keep the 13 sequences** (0 to 12), one stage of the real process each. Shots can be added, cut or renumbered within a sequence; give every shot an id like 7-3.
- **Keep the facts right.** These are already corrected in this draft:
  - quartz is silicon and oxygen joined in a crystal, not loose three-atom molecules;
  - carbon removes the oxygen in the furnace;
  - silicon is purified through a chemical, then deposited onto heated rods, not frozen out of a gas;
  - a seed crystal is pulled from the melt to grow one crystal, the ingot;
  - slicing and polishing make a blank wafer, the foundation for many chips;
  - the fab builds circuits in many repeated operations (deposition, photoresist, lithography, etch, doping, clean, measure);
  - dies are probed on the wafer, then cut apart, packaged and tested.
- **The step count.** Any step count must be tied to the simulated recipe (SMT2020), never presented as true of every chip.
- **Format for each shot.** What the picture shows (one process per shot, with Grain taking part in it or guiding it), the lines, and any on-screen word (one new term at a time). Close-ups of atoms or circuits go inside a magnified "simplified view".
- **Length.** A calm read runs at about 2.5 words a second. Lines are timed from that until they are recorded.
- **Continuity.** Say at every cut what Grain is, who is with it, and how the shot hands off to the next: screen direction, a match cut, or a cut on an action.
- **Hand back** the rewrite as this same file. I turn it into the shot list (`shots.json`), the voice lines (`voice.json`) and each scene's direction.
