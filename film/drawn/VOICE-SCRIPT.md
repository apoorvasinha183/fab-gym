# Sand to Chip: voice script (screenplay v3)

For the scratch read and the recording. Times are a calm read at 2.5 words a second with room for each action; the read settles them. NARRATOR: an interested companion, concrete, room for silence. GRAIN: curious, observant, occasionally dry, never babyish. LOU: a meticulous, kindly robot guide.

## 0-1  (0.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (0.5 s): A handful of sand. Hardly a treasure.
- **GRAIN** (3.8 s): Depends what you find.

## 0-2  (7.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (7.5 s): Come along. There’s something remarkable inside quartz.

## 1-1  (12.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (12.5 s): Many sand grains contain quartz. For this journey, we start with selected quartz rock.

## 1-2  (19.5 s)
- **GRAIN** (20.0 s): What am I made of?

## 1-3  (23.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (24.0 s): Silicon and oxygen, joined in a crystal.

## 2-1  (28.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (29.0 s): In a fiercely hot furnace, carbon helps remove the oxygen.

## 2-2  (34.5 s)
- **GRAIN** (35.0 s): Oh! We’re going different ways.

## 2-3  (38.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (39.0 s): We have silicon. But it isn’t pure enough yet.

## 3-1  (44.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (44.5 s): We convert it into a chemical that’s easier to purify.

## 3-2  (50.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (51.0 s): Distillation separates it from unwanted substances.

## 3-3  (55.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (55.5 s): Another reaction deposits very pure silicon onto heated rods.

## 4-1  (60.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (61.0 s): Next, we melt the purified silicon.

## 4-2  (65.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (65.5 s): A seed crystal touches the melt, then slowly rises and turns.

## 4-3  (71.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (72.0 s): Silicon solidifies onto it, following the seed’s atomic pattern.

## 4-4  (77.0 s)
- **GRAIN** (77.5 s): All that… is me?
- **NARRATOR** (79.6 s): One continuous crystal. An ingot.

## 5-1  (83.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (83.5 s): The ingot is sliced into thin discs.

## 5-2  (88.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (88.5 s): Their surfaces are finished, polished, and carefully cleaned.

## 5-3  (93.0 s)
- **GRAIN** (93.5 s): Am I a chip now?
- **NARRATOR** (96.0 s): A wafer. We’ll build many chips on you.

## 6-1  (100.5 s)
- **LOU** (101.0 s): Welcome to the fab. Precious cargo coming through!

## 6-2  (105.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (106.0 s): Here we build the circuits. Even a speck of dust can spoil the work.

## 7-1  (113.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (113.5 s): First, we add a thin film of material.

## 7-2  (118.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (118.5 s): Then a light-sensitive coating, called photoresist.

## 7-3  (123.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (123.5 s): Light transfers a pattern. Developing opens selected parts of the coating.

## 7-4  (129.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (130.0 s): Etching removes material through those openings.

## 7-5  (134.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (134.5 s): Adding selected atoms changes how parts of the silicon conduct electricity.

## 7-6  (140.5 s)
- **LOU** (141.0 s): Clean. Measure. Check.

## 7-7  (144.5 s)
- **GRAIN** (145.0 s): Again?
- **LOU** (146.5 s): Many times. Each step has a job.

## 7-8  (151.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (151.5 s): Together, these steps build transistors and their connections.

## 8-1  (156.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (157.0 s): One wafer. Many chips, called dies.

## 8-2  (161.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (161.5 s): Inside, transistors control electrical signals: tiny switches working together.

## 9-1  (166.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (167.0 s): Electrical probes test the dies while they’re still on the wafer.

## 9-2  (173.0 s)
- **GRAIN** (173.5 s): Did I pass?
- **LOU** (175.2 s): You passed.

## 10-1  (177.5 s)
- **NARRATOR** (178.0 s): We cut along the spaces between the dies.

## 10-2  (183.0 s)
- **GRAIN** (183.5 s): Where to now?

## 11-1  (187.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (187.5 s): A package protects the die and connects it to the outside world.

## 11-2  (194.0 s)
- **GRAIN** (194.5 s): So I can join in.

## 12-1  (198.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (198.5 s): One final test. Then the chip can become part of a device.

## 12-2  (205.0 s)
- **NARRATOR** (205.5 s): From quartz to silicon. From wafer to working chip.
- **GRAIN** (209.6 s): Quite a lot inside something so small.
