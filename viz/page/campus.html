Gigafab Campus

A floor plan for the SMT2020 fab, optimised for the overhead hoist, then built and run in 3D. Front end in phase 1, copper back end in phase 2, a pad for phase 3.

A gigafab-style layout of our own design from the SMT2020 testbed. Not a real fab, not TSMC's, no real plant data. Every number that places a tool is assumed.

The campus running

Six hours of simulated fab time (day 30, seed 3000, optimised plan) in 60 seconds. White boxes are lots, orange ones hot lots, drawn larger than life. Lots ride the hoist rails at 7 m; towers at bay mouths are lots waiting for a tool. In the Godot tour a dimmed tool is idle, an amber light means maintenance and a red light a breakdown.

The floor plan

What the optimiser bought

The plan moves about 22,800 lots a day between tools at the release rates. Simulated annealing reorders the area blocks along each spine, picks their side and orders the groups inside them, keeping the two sides within 40 m of each other. The metres are exact for these flows. The method is a heuristic, so a better plan may exist.

In the simulator (seeds 3000 to 3009, days 60 to 150, 95% intervals over all five at once) the optimised plan saves 0.30 min on every move. Cycle time, wafers out and WIP show no clear effect. SMT2020's flat 7.5-minute moves give the same cycle time. On this model lots spend their days queueing at the slowest tool groups (the towers in the film), and minutes saved on the hoist are lost in those queues. Real-fab effect: cannot say.
